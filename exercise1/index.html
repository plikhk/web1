Grace Hopper

1906 – 1992 · "Amazing Grace" of computer programming

About Grace Hopper

Grace Hopper was born in New York City in 1906 and died in 1992. She was a mathematician who taught at Vassar College before she joined the U.S. Navy Reserve during World War II. She worked on some of the first computers, like the Harvard Mark I and the UNIVAC I. Her big idea was that people should be able to write programs in words close to English instead of machine code, and this idea led to one of the first compilers and helped create COBOL. She retired from the Navy in 1986 as a rear admiral.

I chose her because a compiler is the thing that lets us write code that humans can read. As a Software Engineering student, I use that idea every day.

Important events

  1. 1906: Born in New York City on December 9.
  2. 1934: Gets a PhD in mathematics from Yale University.
  3. 1944: Starts working on the Harvard Mark I computer in the Navy team of Howard Aiken.
  4. 1952: Finishes the A-0 System, one of the first compiler-like tools.
  5. 1959: Advises the group that designs COBOL, a language built on English words.
  6. 1986: Retires from the Navy as a rear admiral.

Key inventions and contributions

  • A-0 System: an early tool that turned code into machine instructions.
  • FLOW-MATIC: one of the first programming languages with English-like commands.
  • COBOL: her idea of machine-independent languages shaped it, and it is still used today.
  • First computer manual: she is credited with writing the manual for the Harvard Mark I.
  • The first "bug": her team found a moth stuck in a Harvard Mark II relay, and she helped make the word "debugging" famous.
